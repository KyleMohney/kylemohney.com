Callie's Story: Why Rural Ohio Needs Better Wellness Access

A mother's journey finding a home birth midwife in the country, discovering the near-impossibility of accessing quality practitioners—and why Rooted Vitality will be her partner for her next pregnancy

Published December 2024 8 min read Wellness Stories

My name is Callie. I'm 34, and I live in rural Ohio—where rolling hills are beautiful but where finding a midwife or doula feels like searching for water in a desert.

When I found out I was pregnant with my second child, I knew I wanted a different kind of birth experience than my first. The hospital had been efficient, clinical, and cold. This time, I wanted to feel grounded. Connected. Safe. I wanted to give birth at home, in an inflatable pool with just my husband by my side.

But there was one problem: finding someone to attend my birth. In the city, this might be simple. In rural Ohio, it's nearly impossible.

The Search: Nearly Impossible to Find Help in Rural Ohio

I started looking for a midwife in rural Ohio about six months before my due date. I searched online, called clinics, asked around. Nothing. The nearest certified nurse midwife was 45 minutes away in a hospital-based practice—and they didn't do home births. The direct-entry midwives I found online all served a different county. Hours away.

I expanded my search. I called doulas. Three of them had retired or moved. One was available—but she was an hour and a half away, and made it clear that distance was a critical limitation. She couldn't guarantee she'd make it to my birth on time. She offered to be "available for phone support and postpartum visits," but for the actual labor and birth? No.

Weeks of searching led to one lead: a midwife who worked through a friend's network. Not published online. Not in any directory. Someone you had to know someone to know. She was willing to do my home birth—but only as a personal favor. She wasn't taking new clients. She lived 40 minutes away on a good day, in the opposite direction from any hospital.

That was it. That was my only option. One midwife, as a favor.

My Home Birth: When the Midwife Couldn't Arrive in Time

My labor started on an icy winter night. The roads were bad. My husband and I were alone in our home, waiting for the midwife who was running behind because of weather. We filled our inflatable pool with warm water. Our three-year-old played Fallout on the computer in the next room. And I gave birth—to a beautiful daughter—before the midwife arrived.

The midwife showed up 45 minutes after my baby was born, checked that everything was okay, and left.

It was peaceful. It was beautiful. But it wasn't what I'd planned. It wasn't even safe—we'd been lucky. Everything had gone well. But what if something had gone wrong? What if I'd needed help?

The Real Problem: Rural Wellness Access is Broken

After the birth, I realized something crucial: I didn't just need a midwife. I needed an entire ecosystem of support that simply didn't exist within an hour of my home.

I needed a doula who could offer ongoing postpartum support coming weekly, helping me navigate recovery, supporting my emotional processing. I needed a yoga teacher trained in postpartum care. I needed a nutritionist who understood how to nourish a breastfeeding mother. I needed someone trained in postpartum energy work or somatic therapy to help me integrate what my body had just experienced.

What I got instead: my OB's two-minute postpartum checkup at six weeks, which consisted of "How are you feeling?" and "See you in a year."

The gap between what I needed and what existed in my rural Ohio community was heartbreaking. Not because these practitioners don't exist—they do. But they're scattered. Hidden in private networks. Impossible to find if you don't know someone who knows someone.

The Fundamental Problem: Holistic Wellness Marketplace Failure

Here's what I learned: the holistic wellness marketplace is fundamentally broken for people like me.

Practitioners aren't centralized anywhere. There's no way to verify credentials. There's no way to see real reviews from real people. There's no way to understand if someone is truly qualified or just someone with a nice website. And in rural areas, even if such a marketplace existed, finding practitioners within reasonable travel distance is nearly impossible.

I'm planning to get pregnant again. And when I do, I want something different. I want a marketplace for holistic practitioners where I can:

  • Find verified midwives and doulas within 30 minutes of my home
  • Read real reviews from real clients, not marketing copy
  • Understand their credentials and experience level transparently
  • See what other practitioners they recommend (a network effect that actually helps)
  • Book with confidence that I'm getting the support I actually need

That marketplace didn't exist when I needed it. I had to rely on luck, networks, and a midwife doing me a personal favor.

Why Rural Wellness Access Matters Beyond My Story

My experience isn't unique. Across rural America, women are making do. Choosing between no support and insufficient support. Risking outcomes because they can't find qualified practitioners. Settling for practitioners who can't truly meet their needs because of distance.

The holistic wellness industry is thriving—but it's thriving in cities and suburbs. Rural communities are left behind. And the women who most need integrated, whole-person care are the ones least able to access it.

We shouldn't have to rely on favors. We shouldn't have to cross county lines for basic support. We shouldn't have to gamble on practitioners we found through word-of-mouth with no way to verify their qualifications.

This isn't just about birth. It's about every wellness decision. It's about access. It's about equity.

My Third Pregnancy Plan: Using Rooted Vitality

I'm due again in 18 months. This time, I'm going to approach it differently. I'm going to use Rooted Vitality to find practitioners before I'm pregnant. I'm going to build my team in advance—a midwife, a doula, a postpartum yoga teacher, a nutritionist, someone trained in emotional processing work.

I'm going to know exactly who I'm working with, what they've done with other clients, and how they can support my specific birth vision.

And for the first time, I'll feel prepared. Not because I was lucky enough to know someone. Not because a practitioner is willing to do me a favor. But because there's a platform that actually connects rural women with verified, qualified practitioners who can support whole-person wellness.

That's what Rooted Vitality means to me: the difference between leaving wellness to chance and actually planning for the support we deserve.
